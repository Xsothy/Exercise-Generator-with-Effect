Research for ticket #2 (child of map #1). Question: what does Cambodia's Ministry of Education, Youth and Sport (MoEYS) mathematics curriculum cover in each of grades 1–12, as the official curriculum and the national textbooks give it?

Researched 2026-11-26. Every claim cites its source. The **Gaps** section lists what could not be confirmed.

## Structure (frameworks)

- **Strands, grades 1–9 ([C1-9] intro):** Number, Measurement, Geometry, Patterns and Algebra, plus a cross-cutting skill, Reasoning. Each grade's outcome table also has a **Statistics** row, so in practice there are five content strands.
- **Mathematics components ([CF] §5.2):** Numeracy, Measurement, Geometry, Statistics and Probability, Arithmetic, Analytical Mathematics, Trigonometry.
- **Weekly hours ([CF] tables 2–5):**
  - Grades 1–3: 7 hours a week. Grades 4–6: 6 hours. One hour is 40 minutes.
  - Grades 7–9: 7 hours a week of 50 minutes. Note that [C1-9] says 6 lessons a week for grades 4–9, so the two sources disagree for grades 7–9.
  - Grades 10–12, **Science stream**: 6 hours a week.
  - Grades 10–12, **Social Science stream**: 5 hours a week.
- **Streams ([CF] §4):** the framework sets up "a new educational system for the learners of upper secondary education, grade 10 learners, to have choices of three types of further study namely Science, Social Science and Technical Education". Its subject tables give the Science and Social Science streams the **same subjects with different hours**.
- **Textbook levels in grades 11–12 ([T11A] preface, p. 4):** the textbooks are not split by stream. They are split into a **basic level** (កម្រិតមូលដ្ឋាន) and an **advanced level** (កម្រិតខ្ពស់). The advanced preface says: "students who choose advanced mathematics must also study basic-level mathematics to complete it". So the advanced syllabus is basic + advanced. Grade 10 has a single common course in two volumes ([T10a], [T10b]).
- **Curriculum history ([CF] Introduction):** "From 2005 to 2009 … MoEYS developed a Curriculum Policy … a core curriculum for basic general education (grade 1 through grade 9) and core curriculum for upper secondary education (grade 10 through grade 12)". The textbooks above belong to that generation (imprints 2007–2009). The 2015 framework then calls for new textbooks and notes that the in-use mathematics textbooks have "content mistakes" and "lack of coherent progression".

## Where the streams split

- Grades 1–10 are a single common course ([CF] tables 2–5; [T10a]/[T10b] are single-level books).
- Grades 10–12 are formally split into a Science and a Social Science stream. The streams have the same subjects and differ in hours: mathematics gets 6 h/week in the Science stream and 5 h/week in the Social Science stream ([CF] tables 4–5).
- In grades 11–12 the textbooks are split into a **basic** level and an **advanced** level, where advanced = basic + advanced book ([T11A] preface). Grade 11 advanced adds induction, linear transformations, conditional probability, complex numbers and space vectors with lines and planes. Grade 12 advanced adds limits of sequences, inverse trigonometric and irrational functions, volumes and arc length, differential equations, the binomial and normal distributions, the cross product, number theory (divisibility, primes, GCD/LCM) and parametric and polar equations.
- **Not confirmed from a primary source:** that the Science stream takes the advanced level and the Social Science stream the basic level. This pairing is the natural reading, but no MoEYS document seen here states it (see Gaps).

## Gaps and caveats

1. **Grades 1–6 textbooks not seen.** The grade 1–6 content above comes from the official curriculum [C1-9], whose units cite textbook pages but do not give the textbooks' own chapter titles. No online copy of the MoEYS primary mathematics textbooks was found. Grade 1–6 lists are therefore *curriculum units*, not textbook lessons.
2. **[C1-9] is undated** and hosted by ICMI, not MoEYS. Its content matches the 2005–2009 "core curriculum for basic general education" described in [CF]. Some unit hours are blank or "XX" in the source: G1 U19, G4 U3, G6 U19.
3. **Hours conflict for grades 7–9:** [C1-9] gives 6 lessons a week; [CF] (2015) gives 7 hours a week.
4. **Textbook edition and currency.** The grade 7–12 textbooks read are the 2007–2010 generation (imprints seen: grade 10 approved 2007 and printed 2008; grade 7 printed 2009). [CF] (2015) calls for new textbooks. Whether MoEYS has since issued revised mathematics textbooks for any grade, and whether their contents differ, **could not be confirmed**: the MoEYS website (moeys.gov.kh) rejected automated requests ("Request Rejected").
5. **Textbook host is unofficial.** The scans are on a personal WordPress/Blogspot site. The books themselves carry MoEYS and Educational Publishing House imprints, ISBNs and prakas approval numbers, so their content is treated as primary. Their authenticity was judged from the scanned covers and imprint pages only.
6. **2018 detailed syllabi not found.** A 2025 comparative study cites MoEYS (2018) *Detailed Mathematics Syllabus* documents in Khmer, separately for Primary, Lower Secondary, Upper Secondary Science and Upper Secondary Social Science (https://jurnalpendidikan.unisla.ac.id/index.php/VoJ/article/view/1113). No copy was found online. These would be the authoritative source for the current stream-specific content.
7. **Stream ↔ level mapping** (Science = advanced, Social Science = basic) is not stated in any primary source found. A 2018 conference presentation by a Cambodian educator (Ngeth Youdarith, ICER-APEC 2018, crme.kku.ac.th, which was unreachable for a direct read) is reported in search snippets to give 5 h (Science) vs 3 h (Social Science) for grades 11–12. That **conflicts** with [CF]'s 6 vs 5, so it is not relied on.
8. **Translations.** The English chapter names are this note's translations of the Khmer TOC titles. The Khmer original is kept in the tables for grades 7–9. For grades 10–12 the Khmer titles are on the cited TOC pages.
9. **Sub-lesson detail.** For grades 7–12 the lists stop at lesson and section level, the depth the TOCs give. The topics inside each lesson (for example, which factorisation identities grade 8 covers) need the book pages themselves.
